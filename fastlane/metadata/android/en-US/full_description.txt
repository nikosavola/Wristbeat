Wristbeat turns a Wear OS watch into a standard Bluetooth heart rate sensor.

Gym equipment, bike computers, rowing machines and phone apps that ask for a heart rate belt will
find this watch instead, and read the pulse it is already showing.

It is one button. Press it and the watch advertises the standard Bluetooth Heart Rate Service with
your heart rate in it. Press it again to stop. Everything else tells you what the session is doing.
A notification you cannot swipe away, a chip on the watch face, and a page listing every device
reading you.

WHAT IT IS FOR

* Cardio machines that read a heart rate belt over Bluetooth, where a chest strap is one more thing
  to put on and forget to bring.
* Bike computers that show heart rate, without a second sensor on your chest.
* Anything else that speaks the standard Bluetooth heart rate profile.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

* No account, no sign-in, no history, no cloud.
* No phone app and no phone needed. The watch is the sensor, so it works on its own.
* No network access at all. The only thing that leaves the watch is the heart rate, over Bluetooth,
  to devices you have paired with it.

BATTERY

Broadcasting runs in a foreground service, so it keeps going with the screen off and the watch at
your side. Readings are sent as they arrive, and the notification is redrawn only a few times a
minute rather than on every beat.

A watch left to idle reports less often once its screen goes off. A bolt on the receivers page keeps
it awake for the whole session instead. That costs battery, and it is off unless you ask for it.

WEAR OS

Built for Wear OS 3 and later, for round screens, with the bezel and the back gesture both wired to
what the app does. It asks for Bluetooth and heart rate permissions on the first press, and for
notifications so it can show you the session is still running. Refusing notifications costs you the
on-screen reminder and nothing else.

A watch with an optical heart rate sensor is all it needs, and that is every Wear OS watch worth
wearing to the gym.
